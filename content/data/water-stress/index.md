      title: 'What the indicator measures'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        SDG indicator 6.4.2 expresses total freshwater withdrawn by all sectors as a percentage of total renewable freshwater resources, after subtracting environmental flow requirements — the water that must remain in rivers and aquifers to sustain ecosystems.

        Values above 100% mean a country withdraws more than its renewable supply can replace. That deficit is met by depleting fossil groundwater, by desalination, or by drawing on transboundary rivers. None of the three is indefinitely sustainable, and the first is not reversible on any human timescale.

        The standard thresholds are: under 25% no stress, 25–50% low, 50–75% medium, 75–100% high, and above 100% critical.

      title: 'How to read it'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        **National averages conceal severe local scarcity.** Morocco's national figure of 18.8% places it below the stress threshold, yet several of its basins face acute shortage. Aggregation to the country level is the indicator's most significant limitation.

        **Some very high figures reflect desalination, not depletion.** The Gulf states show extreme values because withdrawal is measured against natural renewable supply while much of their water comes from desalination. The number is accurate but describes something other than aquifer collapse.

        **Trajectory matters more than level.** Use the year buttons to see direction. Morocco has moved from 60.5% in 2000 to 18.8% in 2023, largely through improved accounting and efficiency. Algeria has moved the other way, from 85.8% to 144.8%.

        **Northern Africa exceeds 100% as a region.** The only major world region in that position.
