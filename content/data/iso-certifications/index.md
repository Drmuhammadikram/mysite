      title: 'What each standard covers'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        **ISO 9001** — Quality management systems. The oldest and by far the most widely held, with 1.47 million valid certificates.

        **ISO 14001** — Environmental management systems. Requires an organisation to identify its environmental aspects, set objectives, and operate a Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle against them. 676,000 certificates.

        **ISO 45001** — Occupational health and safety. Replaced OHSAS 18001 in 2018. 543,000 certificates.

        **ISO 22000** — Food safety management, built around HACCP principles. 60,000 certificates.

        **ISO 50001** — Energy management systems. The newest of the five and the least widely adopted, at 38,000 certificates, though the most directly relevant to decarbonisation.

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: 'What the survey does not tell you'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        ISO states plainly that the Survey is not a database, and the caution is warranted.

        **Participation is voluntary.** Certification bodies choose whether to report. When a major body withholds data, a country's figures can collapse without anything changing on the ground. China's absence from the 2023 survey caused reported ISO 9001 certificates to fall from 530,904 to 130,402.

        **A certificate is not an outcome.** This is the central problem for research. Certification records that an organisation has a management system meeting the standard. It does not record whether emissions fell, whether waste declined, or whether the system is operated substantively rather than for the audit.

        **Counts favour large economies.** Absolute numbers track industrial base as much as environmental commitment.
