      title: 'How the Index is built'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        The Index is compiled annually by the Sustainable Development Solutions Network and published in the Sustainable Development Report. It draws on roughly a hundred indicators, normalised to a common 0–100 scale where 0 marks the worst observed performance and 100 marks Goal achievement.

        Each of the 17 Goals receives a score, and the overall Index is their arithmetic mean. That equal weighting is a deliberate methodological choice rather than a technical necessity: it treats every Goal as equally important, refusing to assume that economic goals matter more than environmental ones, or the reverse. It is a defensible position, but it is a position, and results should be read with that in mind.

        Use the buttons above the map to switch between the overall score and any individual Goal. The difference between a country's overall rank and its performance on a specific Goal is often where the interesting question lies.

    content:
      title: 'How to read it'
      subtitle: ''
      text: |-
        **Overall scores compress a great deal.** A country scoring 70 might be strong across the board, or excellent on ten Goals and failing on seven. Switching to individual Goals reveals which.

        **High-income countries score well but export their costs.** The Index scores performance within national borders. Goal 12, on responsible consumption and production, is where this shows: several countries near the top of the overall ranking perform poorly on it, because their consumption drives environmental pressure elsewhere.

        **Trajectory is not visible here.** A score is a snapshot. A country improving rapidly from a low base and one stagnating at a similar level look identical on this map.

        **Grey means not included.** Around two dozen countries lack sufficient data coverage for a reliable score.
